## What Is a White Elephant Gift Exchange?

It's a gift swap game with a twist: you can steal. Everyone brings one wrapped present, and the gifts are piled in the middle without names on them. Players take turns choosing a present, opening it in front of everybody, or stealing something already opened by another player.

It's called "white elephant" after an old story about gifts that are more of a burden than a treat. These days nobody minds, because the fun is in the chase. Some people bring useful gifts and others bring silly ones, and a good pile has some of each.

## White Elephant Rules: Step by Step

1. **Set the budget.** Decide a price limit before the party. $15 to $25 is common.
2. **Everyone brings one wrapped gift.** No names, no labels. Put them all in one spot.
3. **Draw numbers.** Everyone picks a number from a hat or bowl. This sets the turn order.
4. **Player 1 goes first.** They choose a gift from the pile and open it so everyone can see.
5. **Player 2 has a choice.** They can open a new gift from the pile or steal Player 1's gift.
6. **If your gift is stolen, you pick again.** You can either open a new gift or steal a different one. In most groups you can't take back the gift that was just taken from you until the next turn.
7. **Keep going in number order.** Each player either opens a wrapped gift or steals.
8. **Player 1 gets a final turn.** Since they went first with the fewest options, they get a last chance to swap with anyone.
9. **Game over.** Everyone keeps the gift in their hands.

That's all there is to it. The rest is just decisions about how competitive you want to be.

## The Steal Rules Most Groups Use

Stealing is what makes white elephant fun, and it's also where most arguments start. Settle these before the first gift is opened:

- **Steal limit:** Most groups allow each gift to be stolen three times. After the third steal, the gift is "frozen," and the last player to take it keeps it. Some choose two steals to speed up the game.
- **No immediate steal-backs:** If someone steals your gift, you can't snatch it right back on the same turn.
- **No stealing a frozen gift:** Once it's frozen, it's safe.
- **Opening is public:** Gifts get unwrapped in front of the whole group, because the reactions are half the show.

Write the rules on a card and put it on the table. It saves a lot of debates.

## How to Choose a White Elephant Gift

The best gifts get people excited enough to steal them. A few categories always work:

- **Useful and cozy:** blankets, mugs, candles, a nice set of snacks.
- **Funny:** a giant pickle pillow, a mystery bag of weird snacks, a "world's okayest" trophy.
- **Practical:** a phone speaker, a small kitchen tool, or a gift card.

Avoid anything personal or too embarrassing. A gift that makes people say "I want that!" will go home with someone different every round, which is exactly what you want.

## 7 White Elephant Variations

Once your group knows the basics, try one of these.

### 1. Yankee Swap

Nearly the same game, but with unlimited steals until the final turn. It's louder and more chaotic, and it's great for groups that like a little drama.

### 2. Dice Roll White Elephant

Pass around a die and a pile of gifts. A common house rule is that an even roll means you steal and an odd roll means you open a new gift. Decide on your own rule and write it down.

### 3. Left, Right Story Game

Hand out wrapped gifts in a circle. Read a short story out loud, and every time you say "left," everyone passes their gift to the left. Every "right" sends the gift the other way. When the story ends, you keep what you hold. Write a story with a lot of "left" and "right" words.

### 4. Themed Gifts

Choose a theme: gifts under $10, snacks only, handmade, "something you already own," or a tea and coffee swap. Themes make shopping easier and bring out the creativity.

### 5. Virtual White Elephant

For friends and family who live far apart, use a video call. Ship gifts ahead of time or have everyone hold up a photo, and use a random number generator for turn order. The winner of each steal mails the gift after the party.

### 6. Re-Gift Edition

Everyone brings something they already own but no longer need. It's the cheapest version, and it often leads to the funniest gifts of the night. Wrap them nicely so nobody can tell what's inside.

### 7. Kids' White Elephant

Use a lower limit ($5 to $10), fewer steals (maybe one), and age-appropriate gifts like stationery or small toys. Keep the game short so nobody gets upset about losing a gift.

## Quick Tips for Hosting

**Start with a clear steal limit.** The number one cause of arguments is two people remembering a rule differently.

**Keep the gift pile visible.** It builds excitement and helps new players understand the game.

**Use a timer for slow decision-makers.** Thirty seconds per turn is usually plenty.

**Have a few backup gifts.** If someone forgets theirs, they can still join in.

**Take photos of each opening.** The faces are priceless.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does white elephant work?

Everyone brings one wrapped gift and draws a number. In number order, each player either opens a wrapped gift from the pile or steals an already-opened gift from someone else. When every gift has been opened, the game ends and everyone keeps what they're holding.

### How many times can a gift be stolen?

Most groups allow three steals per gift. After the third steal the gift is frozen and the person holding it keeps it. Some groups choose two steals, and chaotic groups allow unlimited steals.

### What is the difference between white elephant and Yankee Swap?

The two games are very close. A Yankee Swap usually allows unlimited stealing, with the last player holding each gift keeping it, while white elephant often freezes a gift after a set number of steals. Many people use the two names for the same game.

### What is a good white elephant gift price limit?

$15 to $25 works for most groups. Agree on a number before the party and tell guests early, so nobody spends too much or too little.

### How many people do you need for white elephant?

Six is the minimum for a fun game, and 10 to 20 is the sweet spot. Bigger groups work too, but the game takes longer, so use a two-steal limit to keep it moving.
